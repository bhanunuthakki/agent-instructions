# Pruning

Use this reference when the user asks to cull, consolidate, prioritize, restructure, or turn exploratory work into a coherent retained analysis.

## Classify the work

Inventory completed analyses and classify each as core evidence, supporting evidence that changes interpretation, useful sensitivity or diligence, or exploratory work that no longer earns space. Keep core work prominent, retain supporting work selectively, and remove the last category from the submitted reader path.

For every retained analysis, identify the question it answers, evidence it adds, calculation owner, effect on the conclusion or action, overlap with other work, and remaining limitation.

## Consolidate the model

- Give each reusable calculation one owner.
- Link summaries and outputs to that owner.
- Consolidate schedules that serve the same grain and purpose.
- Preserve separate schedules when they answer distinct questions or need different grains.
- Remove obsolete metrics, definitions, helper columns, charts, named ranges, and navigation links.
- Trace dependencies before deleting or moving dimensions.
- Re-run formulas, checks, chart sources, and hyperlinks after structural changes.

Fewer tabs alone do not make a cleaner model. The goal is less duplicated logic and a clearer reader path.

## Shape the point of view

Use the case brief to decide what the retained work must establish. Do not force every case into the same narrative. The model should make it easy to trace what the data show, why it matters, what conclusion follows, what remains uncertain, and what action or decision follows when requested.

Before moving to the write-up, present the proposed headline, work to retain or remove, planned output views, assumptions and limitations that must survive, and any targeted gap that still blocks the answer. Wait for approval or revision.
